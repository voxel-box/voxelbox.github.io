Prints, run like production.

the problem

What we were handed.

One-off custom parts and gifts sit in an awkward gap: mass production won't touch them, and bespoke fabrication is priced for businesses. Most hobby print services fill the gap badly — no queue, no quality bar, no idea when your part ships.

The printing is the easy half. The hard half is everything around it: intake, scheduling, failure recovery, and telling people what's actually happening with their order.

what we built

The system.

A made-to-order print service run with server-ops discipline. Requests come in as anything from a rough idea to a finished model file, get turned into a printable design, and enter a real queue.

The printers are treated like the rest of our fleet: jobs are monitored, failures are caught and re-run instead of shipped, and the customer hears about progress without having to ask.

Intake

From a rough idea or a model file to a printable design.

Queue

A real schedule — orders don't disappear into a pile.

QC

Failed or flawed prints get caught and re-run, not shipped.

Comms

Status updates along the way, unprompted.

the outcome

What changed.

The same ops discipline we apply to servers, applied to atoms. Custom parts ship predictably, and the service pays its way inside a brand people already trust with their game worlds.
